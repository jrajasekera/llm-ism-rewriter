# Corpus baseline, 2026-11-13

This is the first measurement of the checker against human-written prose that
predates large language models. Every document in `corpus/manifest.json` was
written before November 2022, so each finding below is a candidate false
positive: the checker flagging prose that cannot have picked up the habits it
looks for. These are rates of writing-quality signals on a named sample. They
say nothing about who wrote anything, and no figure here is a per-document
score.

The JSON record of this run is `2026-11-13-baseline.json` in this directory.
The tables in the appendix are the `--format markdown` rendering of the same
run.

## Sample

48 documents, 88,656 scanned prose words, captured 2026-11-13.

| Register | Documents | Prose words | Source types |
|----------|----------:|------------:|--------------|
| `technical-docs` | 13 | 17,668 | 7 at release tags, 5 at commits, 1 RFC |
| `essay-blog` | 12 | 22,994 | 10 Wayback snapshots (4 Gutenberg texts, 6 personal sites), 2 at commits |
| `legal-policy` | 13 | 26,146 | 5 at release tags, 3 at commits, 3 Wayback snapshots (2 Federal Register rules, 1 UK Act), 1 RFC, 1 Wikipedia revision |
| `general-prose` | 10 | 21,848 | 8 Wikipedia revisions, 2 Wayback snapshots of Gutenberg texts |

Long documents were cut to whole blocks within 2,500 words; the manifest's
`extract` field records each cut. No register reaches the 50 documents that
`references/negative-results.md` asks for before a threshold counts as
calibrated, so every recommendation below is directional. A sample of 10 to 13
documents per register can show that a rule misfires on human prose. It cannot
show that a threshold is right.

## Checker state

The run used tool and registry version 2.4.0. It was first taken while the
2.4.0 detectors were still being written, then repeated offline on the
committed 2.4.0 code, and the two JSON records were identical. The three rules
added in 2.4.0 produced no candidate in any document.

## Notable false-positive patterns

**Serial clauses read as padding.** `RH.PHRASE_TRIAD` matches any three
comma-separated runs of two to eight words ending in `and`. That is ordinary
English syntax. Twain's `a discoverable meaning, also a discoverable purpose,
and a show of relevancy` is a deliberate list, as is a startup essay's `You
build something, make it available, and if you've made a better mousetrap`.
Faraday's first candle lecture alone has 12 candidates (5.5 per 1,000 words).
The current trigger, two matches per document, is met by 10 of 12 essays and
26 of all 48 documents at candidate level.

**`Of course,` as a P0 fingerprint.** `FP.META_NARRATION` treats a
sentence-initial `Of course,` as chatbot residue. In this sample it is the
author conceding a point: `Of course, you can trust Homebrew`, and the same
opener in the TypeScript handbook, GitHub's privacy statement, and a 2021
essay. Four documents in three registers, each flagged at the highest priority.

**`period.` read as an aphorism.** The `RH.APHORISM_CLOSER` pattern for a
sentence ending in `period.` fired three times, and all three are the noun:
`is allowed during this period.`, `restarting the six-month timeout period.`,
and `were not in common use during the period.`

**Encyclopedic sentences that start the same way.** `RH.FORCED_SYMMETRY` found
eight paragraphs in five of the ten general-prose documents with three or four
consecutive sentences sharing a first word. The commonest opener is `The`,
which is how an article describes one subject sentence after sentence. In legal
text, at the relaxed threshold of six, it flagged RFC 2026's roadmap paragraph,
a run of 12 sentences of the form `Section 2 describes`, and a Federal Register
summary of six sentences opening with `The`.

**Template syntax read as an unfilled placeholder.** `FP.UNFILLED_PLACEHOLDER`
matched the Liquid tag `{{ page.title }}` in a Jekyll blog post and the Hugo
shortcode `{{% heading "whatsnext" %}}` in the Kubernetes docs, both at P0. The
third match, `[INSERT CONTACT METHOD]` in the Contributor Covenant, is a real
placeholder in a template meant to be filled in, so that one is correct.

Smaller patterns, one or two documents each: `Honestly, no.` and `Frankly,` as
performative honesty; a literal `indelible mark` left by a copying pencil, and
a 2010 `a testament to your creativity`, as inflated significance; and
`not merely … but` and `Not only here, but` as false contrast, in an 1868
lecture among others.

## Threshold recommendations

For each provisional threshold in `references/negative-results.md`. "Effective"
is the threshold after the register's profile relaxes it.

| Rule | Recommendation | Numbers behind it |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| `RH.TRIAD_DENSITY` (> 0.25 per sentence) | Hold; profile carve-out for `legal-policy` | No crossings outside legal. Maxima: essays 0.057, general 0.141, technical 0.370 (effective 0.5). Legal median 0.19, P90 0.59; 3 of 13 cross the relaxed 0.5 (Apache 2.0 at 0.62, Contributor Covenant 0.59, GitHub privacy statement 0.51). Defined-term enumeration is how licences are written. |
| `RH.PHRASE_TRIAD` (≥ 2 per document) | Tighten | Candidates in 33 of 48 documents. A count of 2 is met by 10 of 12 essays, 5 of 10 general, 7 of 13 legal, 4 of 13 technical. Per-document rate: median 0.82 per 1,000 words, P90 2.4, maximum 5.5. A count that ignores length cannot work across 300- and 2,500-word documents; a per-1,000-word rate or a narrower pattern would. Choose the number after a generated sample exists. |
| `RH.FORCED_SYMMETRY` (≥ 3 same-opener sentences) | Tighten for `general-prose` | 8 crossings in 5 of 10 general documents, `The` the commonest opener; paragraph maxima 4 there, 5 in essays (effective 6). Requiring 4, or not counting article openers, would clear most of the general-prose hits. Legal at the relaxed 6: 2 documents, both deliberate roadmaps. |
| `ST.HEADING_DENSITY` (< 60 words per heading) | Hold | No crossings. Lowest observed: 49.6 in technical docs (effective 30), 54.8 in legal (effective 30), 81.4 in general, 371 in essays. |
| `ST.HEADING_FRAME_REPEAT` (≥ 3 headings sharing a first word) | Tighten the detector | General: Wikipedia's `By …` sections (4) and `Ancient …` history sections (6). Technical at the relaxed 6: Node's `emitter…` API headings (12) and Homebrew's `Why …` FAQ questions (9); React's `Step …` reached 5. These are siblings by design. A family carve-out would also drop `ST.HEADING_DENSITY` and `ST.HEADING_RHETORIC`, so prefer ignoring question words and code identifiers as frames. |
| `ST.LABELED_BULLET_RUN` (> 0.5 of ≥ 4 bullets) | Still insufficient data | 41 sections with 4 or more bullets; every ratio is 0 except one at 0.5. The sample has no glossary or API-reference section built from labeled bullets, which is the control the threshold needs. |
| `ST.DASH_DENSITY` (> 1 per 120 words) | Hold | No crossings. Maxima: general 0.0069 per word (83% of the threshold), legal 0.0082 and technical 0.0138 against an effective 0.0167, essays 0.0042. The calibrated version is still the author-relative baseline. |
| `ST.UNIFORM_RHYTHM` (stdev < 4 words) | Hold | No crossings. Lowest standard deviation: 6.8 in essays, 9.0 in general, 9.7 in technical, 13.3 in legal. |
| `ST.FRAGMENT_RUN` (≥ 3 sentences of ≤ 5 words) | Hold | One crossing: an essay's run of one-word questions, `Righteousness? Beauty? Elegance?`, which is the kind of device the rule asks a reader to look at. Technical docs reach 3 under an effective 6, in Homebrew FAQ answers such as `Yes! It's easy!` followed by a sentence that is mostly inline code. No creative or chat controls yet. |
| `ST.SENTENCE_CONTINUITY` (> 0.75 of pairs) | Hold | No crossings. Maxima: essays 0.638, general 0.394. The profiles that skip it peaked at 0.606 (technical) and 0.350 (legal), so this sample neither requires nor rules out those skips. |
| `ST.CROSS_FILE_TEMPLATE` (phrase in ≥ 3 files) | Still insufficient data | Measured across unrelated documents of one register, as a chance floor. Essays and general: no repeated 5-word phrase. Technical: 2 (`let's take a look at`, `at the top of the`). Legal: 4 boilerplate phrases, among them `including but not limited to` in 4 files. Calibration needs sets with an intentional house style, which this corpus does not have. |

## Limits of this sample

- **Size.** 10 to 13 documents per register, all below the bar for a
  calibrated threshold.
- **House style.** Eight of the ten general-prose documents are Wikipedia
  articles, and four of the thirteen legal documents are licence texts.
- **Extraction changes what the checker sees.** Reducing wikitext removes the
  citations, so `Recent studies have shown`, cited in the source, reached the
  checker uncited and became an `LX.VAGUE_AUTHORITY` finding. Converted HTML
  keeps headings and lists but not emphasis, so a Paul Graham section title
  arrives as a one-word paragraph.
- **Parser behaviour, not corpus behaviour.** The parser treats a block quote
  with no attribution cue as prose, so quoted passages in essays were measured.
  It reads indented code blocks as prose, which affects the Homebrew FAQ.
- **Period.** Four of the twelve essays date from 1841 to 1909, and both
  general-prose lectures from the 1860s. That widens the range of human prose,
  and it also puts those registers farther from current writing than a reader
  might assume.
- **No comparison set.** Nothing here measures generated text, so these rates
  cannot show whether a rule separates anything, and they are not meant to.
  They show how often each rule fires on prose that cannot contain the habits
  it targets.

## Reproducing

```bash
python3 scripts/corpus_measure.py --format json --output corpus/results/2026-11-13-baseline.json
python3 scripts/corpus_measure.py --offline --format markdown
```

The first command fetches whatever is missing from `corpus/cache/` and stops
with `INTEGRITY FAILURE` if any byte differs from the manifest. On the capture
date, each of the 26 sources not served from a Git tag or commit was fetched a
second time into an empty cache, and all 26 matched their recorded checksums.
That second pass is how the live govinfo pages were found to change on every
request; they are now pinned through archive snapshots.
