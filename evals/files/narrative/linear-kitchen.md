# Supper

Amira's father had decided to cook, which he had not done since her mother left. He stood at the counter with the recipe propped against the bread bin and read each step aloud before he did it.

"Dice the onion," he said, and diced it, badly.

She offered to help. He said he had it under control. She set the table for two and then, because the table looked bare, got out the candlestick her mother had left behind. He glanced at it and went back to the pan.

The rice caught on the bottom. He scraped the good part into a bowl and left the burnt part to soak. They ate. He asked whether it was all right, and she said it was good, and had a second helping so that he would believe her.

Afterwards he washed and she dried. He held up the burnt pan. "Tomorrow," he said, "I'll try the chicken."
