# The Allotment

When her husband died, Bea kept his allotment on, though she had never grown anything in her life. The committee gave her one season to bring it up to standard.

She dug it in March with his spade, which was too long for her. She planted his seed potatoes in April, three weeks late. In June the man on the next plot, a retired bus driver called Mr Addo, showed her how to earth them up, and told her that her husband had never once earthed up anything and had got by on luck.

In September the committee walked round with a clipboard. They stopped at her plot, and the chairman wrote something down and nodded. After they had gone she lifted the first root. There were nine potatoes on it. She carried them home in her coat pockets and ate two for supper with butter.

She had kept the allotment. In holding on to the plot she had managed to keep the thing her husband had loved, and the season had shown that she could keep it going without him. By passing the inspection she had proved that the allotment would stay hers.
