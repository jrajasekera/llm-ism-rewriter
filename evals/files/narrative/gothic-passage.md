# The Orangery

The orangery at Hallow End had lost its glass to the winters one pane at a time, so that by the year I came to it the ironwork stood open to the sky like the ribs of something the sea had finished with. Ivy had taken the north wall. It had gone in at the ventilators and come out at the roof, and where it crossed the old stove-pipe it had flattened itself against the iron as though listening. Nothing in the beds was alive but the ivy, and a fig, black and enormous, which had split its tub a generation ago and gone down through the tiles to find its own water.

I was shown it by the agent, who stood in the doorway and would not come further. He said the fig had fruited every year since the family left, and that nobody in the village would eat from it. I asked why. He looked at the ivy on the stove-pipe and said that the fruit was perfectly good.
