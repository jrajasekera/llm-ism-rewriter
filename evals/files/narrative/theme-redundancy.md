# The Ferry Ticket

Oskar kept his brother's unused ferry ticket in the lid of the toolbox for nineteen years. He never said why. Hanne thought she knew: to Oskar, throwing a thing away meant agreeing that its owner was not coming back for it.

On Sunday she found him sorting the toolbox on the porch. The ticket lay on the step beside the chisels.

"You could let that go," she said.

"I could." He put it back in the lid. "If I throw it out, it's like saying he won't come for it. Getting rid of it would mean I'd accepted he's gone for good."

She went in to start the soup. For Oskar, discarding the ticket would have amounted to admitting that his brother would never return to claim it, and he was not ready to make that admission.

The soup needed salt. She could hear him on the porch, closing the lid.
