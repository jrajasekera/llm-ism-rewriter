# The Bridge Toll

For thirty years Old Pell took the toll at the footbridge, a penny each way, and gave the pennies to the parish. When the council built the road bridge downstream, nobody used the footbridge any more, and Pell sat in his hut all the same. The village agreed that he was a stubborn man who could not admit his work was finished. He died in the hut in the winter of the floods.

* * *

Forty years later the parish records were moved to the county office, and a clerk there went through the toll ledgers. Pell had entered every penny. In the last eleven years, after the road bridge opened, there were entries on four days in every week. The name beside each was the same, a Mrs Arnott of the far bank, who had been crossing to visit a grave. The clerk checked the burial register. The grave was Pell's wife's. Mrs Arnott was her sister, and the footbridge was the only way across that she could manage on foot.
