# The Night Ferry

The ferry was an hour out when the old man at the rail spoke to her. "You're going back to someone," he said. "People who are leaving look at the wake."

Imke said she had promised her husband she would come back, but that the man she had promised was not the man who was waiting. He had been kind then.

"Then the promise is void," the old man said. "A promise is made to a person. If the person is gone, so is the debt. I was a notary for forty years. A contract with a dissolved company binds nobody."

"It wasn't a contract. I didn't promise because of what he was. I promised because of what I wanted to be, someone who comes back. He can change all he likes. I'm the one who has to live as the person who broke it."

"That's vanity."

"Maybe." She looked at the lights of the harbour they were making for. "But you asked a stranger on a boat, so I think you've broken one yourself, and you'd like me to tell you it was void."

The old man laughed, and did not answer, and after a while he went below.
