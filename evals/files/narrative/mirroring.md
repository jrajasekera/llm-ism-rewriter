# The Boarding House

Lucia took the room at the top of the boarding house in November. The window faced the rail yard, where the freight wagons stood in rows under sodium lamps, and the whole yard had a forsaken look that matched her mood. The radiator knocked twice an hour and gave no heat. It seemed as abandoned as she felt. On the landing the wallpaper had been patched with a different pattern, and even that mismatch seemed to her an echo of her own sense of being out of place everywhere.

She unpacked one suitcase and left the other shut. The landlady had said the last train shunted at eleven and the first at five.
